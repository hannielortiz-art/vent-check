Crashing on the vent? DOPE

Take the patient off the vent, bag with 100% O₂, and call RT and the provider now.

D
Displacement: tube out or in one lung. Check depth, chest rise, EtCO₂.
O
Obstruction: secretions, kink, biting. Suction, check the tube.
P
Pneumothorax: uneven breath sounds, rising pressures, falling BP.
E
Equipment: vent, circuit, O₂ supply. Bagging fixes it if this is the cause.

    –Oxygenation: FiO₂ ≤50% and PEEP ≤8 (SpO₂ ≥88%)

    RSBI is one data point, not the gatekeeper: using it routinely to decide on a breathing trial delayed weaning in a randomized trial. The daily SBT itself is the test.

    Pressure troubleshooting

    High PIP, normal plateau

    An airway problem: something is resisting flow. PIP − plateau is usually under 10.

    • Secretions: suction.
    • Kinked tube or biting: check the tubing; bite block or sedation per order.
    • Bronchospasm: listen for wheezing; the provider may order a bronchodilator.
    • Water in the circuit: drain it away from the patient.

    High PIP and high plateau

    The lungs or chest got stiffer.

    • Pneumothorax: sudden change, uneven breath sounds, falling BP. Urgent.
    • Tube in the right mainstem: check ETT depth and chest rise.
    • Worsening ARDS or pulmonary edema.
    • Tight abdomen: check bladder pressure (IAP).
    • Air trapping or fighting the vent.

    Sudden low PIP

    Usually a leak: the vent can't build pressure.

    • Circuit disconnected.
    • Cuff leak: check cuff pressure.
    • Tube pulled back or out: check depth and EtCO₂.
